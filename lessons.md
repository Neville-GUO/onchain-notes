# Lessons

Things that cost time or opportunity. Written down so they cost it only once.

---

## 1. Register the destination wallet first, not last

Two projects in 2026 followed the same pattern: weeks of testnet activity, then no allocation.

- **Canopy (CNPY)** — testnet ran from February 2026. The claim opened for *registered* wallets only. Activity performed without registering a destination wallet did not resolve to an allocation, even where the on-chain activity existed.
- **GenLayer (GEN)** — Season 1 required registration before the deadline. Points alone were not sufficient; unregistered wallets could not claim regardless of balance.

**The pattern**: the task itself is rarely the blocker. The blocker is a one-minute registration step that nobody schedules, because it does not look like work.

**Rule going forward**: on day one of any campaign, answer three questions in writing —
1. Which wallet will receive the allocation?
2. Is that wallet registered with the project?
3. What is the last date registration is possible?

If all three cannot be answered, the campaign is not started.

---

## 2. "Eligible" and "claimed" are different states

A project can confirm that a wallet is eligible and still not have distributed anything. Confirmation of eligibility is a statement about a list, not about a balance.

Corollary: an open claim page does not mean the claim is live for you. Some claim routes return errors until a wallet is connected — a 404 or a 403 is not proof that a program has ended.

---

## 3. Points are not a promise

A points program is a measure of activity. Whether it converts to a token, at what rate, and under what vesting is a separate decision the project has not necessarily made.

Sort projects by what is written down:

| Written down | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A supply percentage or a conversion formula | The strongest available signal |
| "Points may be considered" | Nothing yet |
| "Points do not guarantee an allocation" | An explicit disclaimer — believe it |

---

## 4. Verify the domain, not the search result

Same-name confusion is not hypothetical:

- A project with a legitimate `app.` subdomain alongside a lookalike root domain running a "pay gas to receive tokens" promotion
- Two unrelated projects sharing a ticker, where one is flagged unverified by exchanges
- A DEX and an unrelated exchange sharing a name across different chains

**Rule**: if a page asks you to send funds to receive tokens, it is not an airdrop. Enter domains by hand from the official docs.

---

## 5. Breadth is not the same as progress

A long list of tracked projects feels like work. It is not, if none of them are being advanced. Nine projects tracked casually produces less than two projects tracked properly.

The question worth asking weekly is not "what am I in" but "what moved this week, and what did it cost me".

---

## 6. Volume-based programs are not free

Programs that reward trading volume require capital, and they charge for it in gas, spread, and slippage. A reward pool shared across thousands of participants rarely covers the cost of qualifying for it.

If a program requires real funds to participate, the correct default is to skip it and say why.
